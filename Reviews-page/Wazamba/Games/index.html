Wazamba Football Betting

Football receives detailed coverage in Wazamba's betting rules. Unless a market states otherwise, standard football bets are based on the result after 90 minutes plus injury or stoppage time. Extra time, penalty shootouts and golden-goal periods are not included in normal 90-minute settlement.

The rules also cover first-half betting. If a match is abandoned during the first half, first-half bets are considered void. If abandonment occurs during the second half, completed first-half bets remain valid.

Postponed, unplayed, abandoned or suspended football matches that are not resumed within 48 hours of their scheduled start are generally treated as void, subject to Wazamba's rules and markets whose outcomes have already been determined.

Football Live Betting Rules

Wazamba allows live football betting through most of a match, including injury time, subject to its own trading decisions. Markets can be suspended during situations that could immediately affect the result of a wager, including dangerous free kicks, penalties and play around the penalty area.

Wazamba also reserves the right to void affected bets when a market remains open after events such as goals, red or yellow-red cards and penalties. The rules additionally allow affected markets to be cancelled when an incorrect score is displayed or when the match time used for offered odds is incorrect by more than five minutes.

Football Goalscorer, Card and Corner Markets

Wazamba documents separate settlement rules for goalscorer, booking and corner markets. Goalscorer bets generally apply to regular time, and own goals are not attributed to a listed goalscorer.

For booking markets, a yellow card counts as one card and a red card as two. A player receiving a yellow followed by a second yellow and subsequent red can therefore account for three cards under the documented settlement method.

Wazamba also uses a points system for some booking markets: a yellow card counts as 10 points, while a red or second-yellow/red counts as 25 points. The documented maximum for a single player is 35 booking points per match.

For corner betting, a corner must actually be taken to count for settlement. A corner awarded but not taken does not count.

Wazamba Esports Betting

Wazamba includes a dedicated E-sports category within its sportsbook. The live interface we reviewed also displayed several esports and simulated football competitions, while the betting rules contain a separate section specifically covering esports settlement.

Wazamba defines esports Moneyline betting as selecting the team or individual competitor that wins the match-up. Tournament Winner markets refer to the team or individual that wins the final of the relevant esports tournament.

Esports markets are settled using the official results declared by the relevant esports association or organizing body. Wazamba states that later disqualifications or subsequent changes to those results are not considered for betting settlement.

Postponed, Interrupted and Replayed Esports Matches

If an esports match is postponed, bets remain valid if the match resumes within 48 hours of the original schedule.

If a match starts but cannot be completed because of a player disconnection or power interruption, Wazamba states that the bets are void. If the match is replayed because of a draw, disconnection or power interruption, bets on the original match are also void and the replay is treated as a new betting market.

A change to the scheduled number of rounds or maps can also affect settlement. In this situation, Handicap and Over/Under bets are considered void, while Moneyline bets remain valid.

Counter-Strike Betting Rules

Wazamba provides specific Counter-Strike rules within its esports section. Where overtime can be played, it is included in market settlement unless a Draw selection is specifically offered for that market.

Most map bets use the scheduled number of rounds and exclude extra rounds played because of a draw. However, if a Map Winner market is offered without a Draw selection, settlement is based on the overall winner of the map, including overtime when played.

The rules also define Counter-Strike-specific concepts including T/CT sides, rounds, overtime and pistol rounds. Wazamba states that the first team to reach 13 rounds wins the map under the rules supplied, while overtime can continue through additional periods until a winner is determined. Tournament-specific overtime rules can still apply.
